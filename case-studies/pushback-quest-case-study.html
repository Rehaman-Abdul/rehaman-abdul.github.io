Pushback Quest

Survive one work week without burning out or burning bridges. A chat-based simulator for practising how to say no, negotiate trade-offs and hold boundaries.

Role
Instructional design, writing, UX & development
Audience
Working professionals in eight roles
Play time
5–8 minutes per week
Built with
HTML, CSS, JavaScript (no frameworks)
Play full screen ↗Design rationale

Best on desktop or tablet. Use "Play full screen" on a phone.

The problem

People get unrealistic, high-pressure requests from managers, peers, clients and executives. Because of the power gap they usually respond in one of two weak ways: passive compliance, which leads to burnout and lower quality, or blunt refusal, which damages trust. The gap is rarely knowledge. It's behaviour under social pressure, so the solution is realistic practice, not a slide deck of tips.

Learning objective: Assertive Alignment

Players practise four behaviours:

  1. Trade-off framing: "I can do X. To keep the date, should we drop Y or move Z?"
  2. Clarify before committing: ask questions that reveal real effort and priorities.
  3. Impact-first language: explain pushback as business risk, not personal inconvenience.
  4. Firm boundaries: a respectful no, with a reason and an alternative, when something breaks policy, security or ethics.

How it works

The player picks a role and lives through one work week (Monday, Wednesday, Friday) inside a fictional chat app, choosing how to reply to each message. Consequences surface later in the week and a role-aware debrief closes the game.

Three meters

Trust, Bandwidth and Quality react to every reply, so players see that each response has several costs at once.

Insights

Asking clarifying questions earns a pinned note that unlocks the strongest reply on Friday.

Escalation & recovery

Blunt refusals bring in the requester's manager; only a strong assertive reply repairs it.

Delayed consequences

Small yeses early in the week show up as a "running on empty" event before Friday.

Boundary moments

When a request breaks policy, security or ethics, the right answer is a firm, respectful no with an alternative.

Role-specific scenarios

Eight roles, from Sales to Finance, each with their own Wednesday dilemma.

Design decisions

  • Feedback in two layers. The story reacts immediately; the coaching ("why it worked", plus an assertive rewrite of your costliest mistake) waits for the debrief so the game doesn't feel like a quiz.
  • Plausible wrong answers. Passive and aggressive options sound like what a capable person might say on a bad day.
  • Accessible by design. No timers, full keyboard play, text and numbers alongside colour, reduced-motion support, WCAG 2.2 AA contrast.
  • Replayable. Three difficulty levels, shuffled cards and random events, with 5–8 minute runs.
  • Private. No backend, accounts, tracking or AI features. Progress is stored only in the browser.

Limitations and next steps

The game has not yet been evaluated with learners, so I make no claims about behaviour change. Planned next steps are expert review of the scenarios by practitioners, think-aloud sessions, and a pre/post test using scenarios that aren't in the game.
